There is a particular kind of marketing email that I have started to dread. You have seen it. The subject line is personalised with your first name. The opening line mentions something vaguely relevant to your industry. The body is structured, clear, even helpful. And yet somehow, reading it, you feel nothing.

Technically excellent. Humanly absent.

This is the challenge of modern marketing. We have tools now that can personalise at scale, automate every touchpoint, test every variable, and deliver messages with surgical precision. And in doing all of that efficiently, we have managed to strip out the one thing that made marketing work in the first place: the sense that another human being is trying to reach you.

## The Efficiency Trap

Marketing automation is genuinely useful. I am not making a case against it. Email sequences, retargeting, CRM triggers, A/B testing — these are legitimate tools that save time and improve consistency.

The trap is confusing efficiency with effectiveness.

You can automate every touchpoint in your customer journey and still fail. Because automation handles the *when* and the *what*, but it cannot handle the *why* — the reason a brand actually earns someone's attention and trust.

That *why* is still human.

## What "Human Touch" Actually Means in Marketing

When I say human touch, I don't mean adding emojis to your emails or writing in a casual tone. Those are stylistic choices, and they may or may not suit your brand.

Human touch in marketing means:

**Acknowledging that your customer is a whole person.** They have pressures you don't see, priorities that shift, and emotions that influence every buying decision. Marketing that respects this complexity will always outperform marketing that treats customers like profiles in a database.

**Having a real point of view.** The brands I admire most say things that not everyone agrees with. They take positions. They express preferences. They have taste. Generic marketing from a company with no discernible personality is almost impossible to remember.

**Writing like you mean it.** You can tell the difference between content written by someone who genuinely cares about the subject and content written to fill a content calendar. So can your customers.

## The Trust Gap in Modern Marketing

Trust in brands has been declining for years. People are more sceptical, more discerning, and more likely to ignore marketing messages than they have ever been.

But something interesting happens when a brand behaves like a person instead of a machine. When you get a response that is clearly written just for you. When a company publicly admits it got something wrong. When a founder shares something vulnerable and real about why they built what they built.

People pay attention. And they trust.

## What This Looks Like in Practice

I have seen this play out at every scale — from small businesses to large enterprises. The principles are the same:

1. **Write to one person.** When you create any piece of marketing, imagine one specific person reading it. Not a demographic. A person. This changes the words you choose, the examples you use, and the problem you center.

2. **Let your team have voices.** Some of the most effective B2B content comes from employees sharing genuine expertise — not polished marketing copy, but real opinions from real people.

3. **Slow down in unexpected places.** Not every touchpoint needs to be automated. Sometimes a personal follow-up email, written by a human, at exactly the right moment, is the most powerful marketing tool you have.

4. **Tell stories.** Data tells people what. Stories tell them why. And why is what they remember.

## The Paradox

Here is what I have come to believe after 28 years: the more powerful our marketing tools become, the more valuable genuine human connection becomes.

Automation raises the average. Human connection creates the outliers — the customers who stay for years, who refer others, who become advocates. That is the business you are actually trying to build.

Do not let the tools make you forget what marketing is really for.
