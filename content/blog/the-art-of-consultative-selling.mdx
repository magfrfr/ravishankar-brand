I have watched thousands of sales calls in my career. Sat through presentations, coached reps, reviewed recordings, and occasionally made the calls myself. And if there is one thing I have noticed that separates the good from the great, it is this:

**The great ones are mostly asking questions.**

They are not presenting. They are not pitching. They are not running through a slide deck while the customer checks their email. They are curious — genuinely, almost irritatingly curious — about the person on the other side of the table.

## The Problem with Traditional Selling

Most sales training still teaches what I would call "conviction selling." The idea is simple: if you believe in your product hard enough, and you communicate that belief with enough enthusiasm and the right features, the customer will say yes.

It rarely works the way it's supposed to. Conviction without context is just noise.

The customer doesn't care how excited you are about your product. They care about their own problem. And until you understand their problem at a level deeper than they have articulated it themselves — you are just another vendor talking at them.

## What Consultative Selling Actually Looks Like

Consultative selling is not a technique. It is a posture. It is showing up to every conversation with the genuine intention to understand before you attempt to be understood.

In practice, that looks like:

**More questions, fewer statements.** Not interrogation — curiosity. "Tell me more about that." "What happens when...?" "How has that affected the team?" These are not manipulation tactics. They are the natural questions a trusted advisor would ask.

**Silence that you don't rush to fill.** Most salespeople are terrified of silence. They fill it with more pitch. The best consultative sellers let silence work. A customer thinking means a customer engaging.

**Recommendations that fit, not push.** If your product is not the right fit for this customer's problem, say so. I have won more long-term business by telling a prospect "I don't think we're the right solution for you right now" than by closing a deal that was wrong for both parties.

## The Trust Equation

There is a simple equation that drives consultative selling:

**Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) ÷ Self-Orientation**

The more you are focused on your own numbers, your own quota, your own commission — the less trustworthy you appear, even when you don't say it out loud. Customers are remarkably good at sensing when someone is selling *at* them versus *with* them.

Low self-orientation doesn't mean you don't care about your results. It means your results come from caring genuinely about theirs.

## A Story That Stayed With Me

Early in my career, I had a mentor who refused to let me start any customer meeting by talking about our product. His rule: the first fifteen minutes belong to the customer. Ask. Listen. Take notes. Then — if and only if something you offer might actually help — you can speak.

I thought it was slow. Inefficient. I wanted to get to the pitch.

He was right. I was wrong.

The slowest way to sell is to start talking before you understand. The fastest way is to understand so thoroughly that the customer starts convincing themselves.

## Where to Start

If you want to move toward consultative selling, start with one shift: **enter every sales conversation with one goal — to understand the customer better than they expect to be understood.**

Not to close. Not to present. Not to handle objections. Just to understand.

The rest follows naturally from that.
