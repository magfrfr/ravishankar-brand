I have seen a lot of sales organisations in my career. Built some, joined some, and occasionally helped repair ones that had quietly fallen apart. And the pattern I have observed, again and again, is this:

**Most sales cultures are built for the short term, then surprised when they do not last.**

The leader wants results now. The incentives are structured for results now. The hiring, the onboarding, the management — all of it optimised for now. And it works. For a while. Then the best people leave, performance plateaus, and everyone wonders what happened.

What happened is that the culture was never really designed for people. It was designed for output.

## Culture Is Not What You Say It Is

Every sales team I have ever encountered believes it has a good culture. Ask any leader. They will tell you about their values, their team spirit, their "family" atmosphere.

Then talk to the reps. Listen to what they actually experience day to day. The story is usually quite different.

Culture is not what leadership says it is. It is what the people on the team experience consistently, especially when things are hard.

When a rep misses a month, are they supported or are they shamed? When a top performer uses tactics that feel off, is that addressed or ignored because the numbers look good? When someone raises a concern about a process that is not working, do they feel heard?

Those moments define culture. Not the all-hands slides.

## What a Lasting Sales Culture Is Built On

In my experience, the sales cultures that endure — that attract and retain great people, that perform consistently over years, not quarters — share a handful of characteristics.

**They hire for character, not just competence.** Skills can be taught. Integrity, curiosity, and genuine care for the customer are much harder to develop in someone who does not already have them. The best sales teams I have been part of had people who were good human beings first and good closers second.

**They value long-term relationships over short-term wins.** This sounds obvious, but the incentive structure of most sales organisations punishes it. If your commission plan rewards closing at all costs, that is what you will get — closing at all costs. Build incentives that reward retention, referrals, and customer success alongside initial revenue.

**They give people room to learn from failure.** Fear of failure makes salespeople play it safe, avoid difficult prospects, and pad their pipelines with deals that will never close. Cultures that treat failure as data — not as a reason for shame — end up with reps who take smart risks and improve faster.

**Leadership sells too.** I have a rule: I do not expect anything of my team that I am not willing to do myself. If cold outreach is hard and unglamorous, I do it too. If a big presentation matters, I am in the room, not behind a glass wall. Nothing erodes a sales culture faster than leaders who manage from a distance.

## The Manager is the Culture

There is a saying I come back to often: *people don't leave companies, they leave managers.*

In sales, this is almost universally true. A great manager can build a high-performing culture within a mediocre company. A poor manager can destroy a culture that leadership has spent years building.

This means that the most important investment you can make in your sales culture is in the quality of your frontline managers. Not just their sales skills — their coaching ability, their emotional intelligence, their capacity to give honest feedback with genuine care.

If you want a culture that lasts, develop your managers first.

## Where to Start

If you are leading a sales team and you want to build something that lasts longer than a few good quarters, I would start here:

1. **Ask your team how they actually experience the culture.** Not in a survey that goes to your inbox. In a conversation. And really listen.

2. **Look at your incentive structure.** What behaviour does it actually reward? Is that aligned with the culture you want?

3. **Identify your best managers and learn from them.** What are they doing that creates loyalty? How do they handle the hard conversations?

4. **Set a standard for how the team treats customers.** Not a mission statement — a real, observable standard that leadership holds itself to as much as anyone else.

Sales culture is built slowly and lost quickly. The leaders who understand that are the ones who build teams that last.
